[ ../ ](/) # Human Voices With most content now AI-generated, writing without it matters more than ever. This blog is where I find my own voice. Writing is thinking — without it, I'd stop evolving. ## Writing Every time I sit down to write, I do it for me. Long flights, busy cafes — wherever. I'm overcoming a tendency to consume and, instead, producing something. Creativity flows like a faucet. You have to let it run a while before anything clear comes through. That's why I write as much as I can, across topics from biology to economics. Writing distills thinking. It's only while writing that I dive deep enough to develop my own ideas and my own bet on what the future looks like — shaping the world instead of being shaped by it. ## Your voice Writing will feel awkward at first. It might always. But the more you do it, the more you develop your own voice — sharp and dry, or playful. A good idea, written down, has zero marginal cost to spread. Your voice becomes your personal brand: a way to connect with readers, share what you know, and make the world a bit less siloed. ## Own it The internet was built on collaboration and openness. Today it's mostly monetized, walled, or optimized for engagement. Social media users perform instead of being — adopting no one's voice to chase visibility. It doesn't have to be that way. Cultivate your own corner of the internet. For me that means open formats (Markdown), open-source software (Jekyll), static hosting, RSS, and owning my domain. My voice stays mine while reaching whoever wants to hear it. ## On AI Using AI to write strips you of your own voice. I write all posts myself — no AI drafts, no rewrites. When I'm done, I'll ask it to flag typos or awkward phrasing, then manually decide what to keep. I also use it to quickly validate assumptions. It would be a disadvantage not to understand its strengths and weaknesses.
